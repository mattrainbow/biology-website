Building the upper beak of a Darwin's finch

A progress zone of dividing cells pushes the beak out from the face (left) towards its tip. Behind it, cells stop dividing, crawl and sort themselves into four layers: epithelium on the outside, then a vascular layer, then dermis, with bone at the core.

What you're looking at

  • Progress-zone cells are dark gray, growing and dividing at the leading edge.
  • Epithelium is the outer sheet, flattened along the surface.
  • Sensory neurons sit in the epithelium. Spiky dendrites reach towards the surface; one thicker axon runs inward and branches at its end.
  • Pigment cells are black (melanin) or yellow.
  • Capillary beds carry blood in red from an arteriole. It gives up its oxygen and leaves blue through a venule.
  • Dermal fibroblasts crawl like amoebae while light-tan collagen appears around them.
  • Bone cells crawl into place and settle as osteocytes in darker-tan bone collagen.
